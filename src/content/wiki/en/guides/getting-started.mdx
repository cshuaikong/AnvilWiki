## What is First Person Boxing on Roblox?

First Person Boxing is a Roblox fighting experience presented from your boxer’s point of view. The official listing describes punches, slips, counters, separate head and body guards, stamina management, knockdowns, and replays. It supports matches against players and practice against AI, giving beginners a place to learn before relying on a tactic in live competition.

The experience is published under **Can You Box?** and supports PC, mobile, and console. Use the [official Roblox experience](https://www.roblox.com/games/132956556682909/First-Person-Boxing) as the launch point so you do not land in another boxing game with a similar name.

## What should you do before your first match?

Check the prompts shown on your own device before trying to memorize a guide. The public game description confirms the actions that matter, but it does not publish a permanent key map. Begin in AI practice when available, identify each control, and confirm that the prompt still matches after a Roblox client or game update.

Use this order:

1. Move and turn until you can keep the opponent in view.
2. Find the punch, head-guard, body-guard, and slip controls.
3. Perform each action separately and observe the stamina display.
4. Test a short defensive response followed by one attack.
5. Recheck the prompts before moving to player matches.

The [controls overview](/controls/controls-basics/) explains each action group. Use the separate [PC controls guide](/controls/pc/) or [mobile controls guide](/controls/mobile/) for device-specific verification.

## Which mechanics matter most to a beginner?

Five systems form the verified foundation: first-person positioning, guards, slips, stamina, and counters. Treat them as connected decisions instead of isolated buttons. Your position affects what you can see, your guard protects a target area, a slip attempts to avoid a punch, and stamina affects how freely you can continue acting.

| Mechanic | What the official description confirms | First practice goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-person view | The fight is shown from behind your gloves | Keep the opponent centered without rushing |
| Head/body guards | Different guards protect different target areas | Identify both current inputs and their visual feedback |
| Slips | Slips are part of the combat system | Recognize the animation and recovery on your device |
| Stamina | Getting tired slows you down | Notice which repeated actions change the meter |
| Counters | Counters are a featured mechanic | Respond only after a clear defensive read |

For a closer explanation of visual awareness, read the [First Person Boxing POV guide](/guides/first-person-boxing-pov/). The [combat basics page](/combat/combat-basics/) connects the same systems during an exchange.

## How should you use guards and slips?

Use the in-game feedback to learn what each defensive choice actually covers. The official description confirms separate protection for the head and body, but it does not publish damage reduction, stamina costs, or invulnerability frames. A reliable guide should therefore help you observe the situation without inventing exact values.

In practice, repeat one scenario at a time: let the AI threaten a target, select the matching guard, then compare that result with a slip. Record what you see after an update instead of assuming old timings still apply. The [guards and slips guide](/combat/guards-and-slips/) provides a focused checklist.

## How do you avoid wasting stamina?

Watch the stamina display while repeating controlled actions, then pause and see how recovery behaves. The official description states that stamina must be managed and that fatigue slows the player, but no official public table gives exact costs. Short tests are more useful than copying a percentage from an unsourced wiki.

The [stamina guide](/combat/stamina/) shows how to test the system without claiming fixed numbers. Once you can recognize when your options narrow, use the [counter and knockout guide](/combat/counters-and-knockouts/) to decide when an opening is worth taking.

## When are you ready for player matches?

Move into PvP when you can identify your current controls, protect both target areas, perform a deliberate slip, and finish a short practice exchange without losing track of stamina. This does not guarantee a win; it means you can learn from an opponent instead of fighting the interface.

After each match, review one question: Did you lose sight of the opponent, choose the wrong guard, spend stamina without a clear purpose, or attack without a readable opening? That answer gives you one focused drill for the next AI practice session.
